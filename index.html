🔗 11A.4 Rate Equations & Mechanisms

Made by Aasia Khawar

A reaction is a story told in steps. The rate equation is the clue that tells you which step is the plot twist.Edexcel IAL Chemistry · Paper 4 · Spec 11.6–11.9, CP9A, CP9B · Questions solved: 0 / 9

1💥 One collision or many steps?

Scene 1 – Does the equation on paper tell the whole story? Choose a reaction and test it.

ElementaryA single collision between the reactant particles. Rate equation can be read straight from the balanced equation.
MechanismA series of connected elementary steps. Intermediates (e.g. Cl•, •CH₃, NO₃) appear in steps but not in the overall equation.
🎯 Golden rule: rate equations come from EXPERIMENTS. If experiment ≠ stoichiometry, the reaction is almost certainly not elementary.

2🚧 The slowest step is the bottleneck

Scene 2 – Like notes being collected, tidied, stapled: the slow collector decides the output. Slide the speed of step 1.

Order = number of particlesrate = k[NO₂]² → two NO₂ molecules are in the rate-determining step (RDS).
In or before the RDSEvery species that takes part in or before the RDS appears in the rate equation. Species after it don’t (zero order).
Steps must add upAdd all steps and cancel intermediates – you must get the overall equation.

3🕵️ Detective: which mechanism fits?

Scene 3 – NO₂ + CO → NO + CO₂ has experimental rate = k[NO₂]². Test each proposed mechanism with two checks: ① do the steps add up? ② does the slow step give the observed rate equation?

⚠️ A mechanism that fits the data is only consistent with it – not proved. More experiments are needed to choose between B and C.

4🧪 Halogenoalkane hydrolysis: SN1 vs SN2

Scene 4 – The rate equation reveals the mechanism. Toggle and follow the ball.

SN1 – unimolecularrate = k[(CH₃)₃CCl]. Zero order in OH⁻. Slow: (CH₃)₃CCl → (CH₃)₃C⁺ + Cl⁻. Fast: carbocation + OH⁻.
SN2 – bimolecularrate = k[RCl][OH⁻]. First order in each, so one particle of each in the RDS. One step, transition state, no intermediate.

5⚗️ Core practical CP9A: iodination of propanone

Scene 5 – CH₃COCH₃ + I₂ → CH₃COCH₂I + H⁺ + I⁻ (acid-catalysed). Use a large excess of propanone and acid, quench samples with NaHCO₃, titrate iodine with thiosulfate. Change the sliders and watch the graph.

ResultsZero order in I₂, first order in propanone and in H⁺: rate = k[CH₃COCH₃][H⁺].
MechanismH⁺ adds to propanone (fast) → slow loss of H⁺ gives an enol → fast reaction of enol with I₂. I₂ comes after the RDS, hence zero order.
Testing itWider concentration range (at very low [I₂] order becomes first), NMR detects the enol, CD₃COCD₃ reacts slower (C–D stronger).
Exam hint: a limitation is the time delay between pipetting and quenching.
